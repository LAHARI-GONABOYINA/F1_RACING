TECHNICAL VS PERFORMANCE

Comparing Formula 1 technical changes with team performance

What We Did

This stage combines the technical specification data with the performance data to examine whether changes in technical attributes are associated with changes in Formula 1 team performance.

Analysis Process

01. Combine Datasets

Technical and performance datasets were combined using the relevant season and team information.

02. Select Variables

Technical attributes such as car weight were compared with performance measures such as championship points and wins.

03. Calculate Changes

Percentage and numerical changes were calculated between the available seasons.

04. Compare Results

Technical changes were compared with changes in team performance.

05. Visualize Relationships

Charts were created to make the relationship between technical and performance measures easier to understand.

06. Interpret Carefully

The results were interpreted as observed associations rather than proof of direct causation.

Observed Relationship

Between the analyzed McLaren seasons, team championship points increased from 666 to 833.

Race wins also increased from 6 to 14.

During the same period, the recorded car weight changed from 798 kg to 800 kg.

This shows that a technical change and performance improvement occurred during the same period.

Important Interpretation

The observed changes do not prove that the technical change caused the performance improvement.

Only two McLaren seasons were available for this comparison, which is not enough to establish a reliable statistical relationship or causation.

Other factors such as driver performance, reliability, race strategy, regulations, competitors and other technical developments may also influence results.

Conclusion

McLaren showed a substantial improvement in measured performance from 2024 to 2025.

Championship points increased by 25.08%, while race wins increased by 133.33%.

The recorded car weight increased by only 2 kg, while other common numerical technical specifications showed no measurable change.

Therefore, the analysis identifies an association between the observed technical and performance changes, but it does not establish that the technical changes directly caused the performance improvement.
